# Goal design standard

Use this reference to separate direction from commitment and measurement.

## Objective, goal, and measure

| Layer | Job | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Names the broad desired condition | Does it explain where to go and why it matters? |
| Goal | Defines a specific result within a time horizon | Can an owner be accountable for an observable result by a date? |
| Measure | Shows movement or final performance | Are the unit, baseline, target, and source of truth clear? |

An objective can guide several goals. A goal can use several measures. Do not collapse all three into a vague sentence.

## SMART test

- **Specific:** the outcome is understandable without hidden context.
- **Measurable:** the measure, unit, baseline, target, and source are named.
- **Achievable:** the user supplies a credible mechanism, constraint check, or decision that the target is acceptable.
- **Relevant:** the goal has a stated connection to an objective or need.
- **Timely:** the completion date and review cadence are explicit.

Do not certify achievable from intuition. If feasibility evidence is missing, state `Achievability decision needed` and identify the decision owner.

## Lead and lag measures

A lag measure records the result after work has taken effect. A lead measure tracks a behavior or condition that the owner can influence earlier.

| Question | Lead measure | Lag measure |
|---|---|---|
| What can the owner change during the period? | [Controllable activity or condition] | Not applicable |
| What result must ultimately move? | Not applicable | [Outcome] |
| When is the signal available? | [Early cadence] | [Result cadence] |
| What is the source of truth? | [Source needed] | [Source needed] |

Activity is not automatically a useful lead measure. It must have a plausible relationship to the outcome, and that relationship should be labeled as evidence or hypothesis.

## Field order

Draft a goal only after checking:

1. objective and reason;
2. owner;
3. outcome;
4. measure and unit;
5. baseline;
6. target;
7. due date;
8. source of truth;
9. lead indicators;
10. dependencies and tradeoffs.

Keep any unknown field visible in the final artifact.

## Build a goal set

- Invite proposals from people closest to the work.
- Debate assumptions, dependencies, and constraints before wording.
- Combine goals that measure the same outcome.
- Limit the set to what can receive real attention and review.
- Force an order instead of using tied priorities.
- Record what was deferred and why.
- Assign an owner and decision date for unresolved tradeoffs.

Do not use a neat ranking as a substitute for judgment. If the input cannot support the order, label the ranking as a decision needed.
